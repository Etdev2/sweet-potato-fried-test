# Greg, Fred, and the sort order

This is an invented workplace comedy. Greg and Fred are fictional characters. The firing, intoxication, mailroom incident, dialogue, and spreadsheet errors below are invented. No actual employee or workplace allegation is described.

Greg presented his forecast in the large conference room because the small one could not accommodate his confidence.

The first slide had a dollar sign, six zeroes, and no date. The second had a photograph of a stadium. Greg considered these mutually corroborating sources.

Fred asked which money belonged to the player, which belonged to the club, and which belonged to the advertiser.

"Commercial money," Greg said.

Fred opened three worksheets.

"You've made it complicated," Greg said.

"I've made it belong to people," Fred replied.

In this fictional agency, management had already asked Greg to correct his model twice. He had instead changed the chart colors. After the third review, the agency terminated his employment.

Greg arrived at reception and caused a scene. He raised his voice and demanded that management "restate the ending." Staff asked him to leave. The ending remained final.

Greg packed a mug marked "Directionally Correct." The handle pointed left.

The following morning, Greg returned to the agency uninvited and drunk. He entered the mailroom and insisted he could sort letters because envelopes did not contain formulas. A few minutes later, he vomited beside the mail sorter.

The mailroom supervisor stopped the machine. Someone brought water. Colleagues helped Greg leave and arranged a safe ride home. Nobody photographed him. He remained a former employee.

Fred covered the remaining sorting. He put a letter addressed to Accounts Receivable in Accounts Receivable. This passed the morning's most demanding attribution test.

Later, the manager prepared a repair plan for the remaining team. Every model would name its recipient, date, source, and assumptions. Greg's agency employment had ended; the team's review obligations continued.

Greg's mug stayed on the shelf. His forecast did not.

The comedy is invented. The analytical lesson is straightforward: different recipients and valuation dates require different records. A spreadsheet can be corrected without turning a person's worst day into office entertainment.
