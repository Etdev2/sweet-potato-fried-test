# The Greg blunder: the $700 million fumble

Optional Grok-authored written draft for a YouTube sports-news parody. This is not a rendered video or a required ST-1 deck. Runtime about 10 minutes at roughly 150 spoken words per minute. Fictional comedy about an exercise character. Real figures cite [the evidence register](../research/evidence.md) by E-ID in the footnotes. Codex corrected valuation and attribution wording after the incoming draft. The original is retained at commit 15970db247359b2cd2ca211691a9ed9bb145c0e4.

Cast: ANCHOR, NARRATOR (voice-over), GREG, FRED (accounting bot), EXEC (Angels executive), ANALYST, FANS 1 to 5, TOURIST.

---

## [0:00] Cold open

*Breaking-news sting. Red ticker: "BREAKING: ANGELS FRONT OFFICE IN CHAOS." ANCHOR sits at a glass desk, holding a single sheet of paper like it might bite.*

ANCHOR: Good evening. Tonight's story isn't a trade or an injury. It's an accountant. His name is Greg, and he may have made the most expensive mistake in sports history. Twice.

*Beat. ANCHOR puts the paper down.*

ANCHOR: We checked the math three times. Our accountant checked it a fourth time. Our accountant is a robot.

*Title card slams in: THE GREG BLUNDER. Drumline hit.*

## [0:30] Who is Shohei Ohtani?

*B-roll: home runs, strikeouts, a packed Dodger Stadium.*

NARRATOR: To understand the blunder, you have to understand the player. Shohei Ohtani hits. He also pitches. In 2024 he hit 54 home runs and stole 59 bases. In 2025 he hit 55 more and went back to the mound.¹

*Graphic: "4 MVP awards. All unanimous."²*

ANALYST: *(at a tactics board, drawing one circle around two players)* Most teams pay one star to hit and another to pitch. The Dodgers pay one man to do both, and he uses one roster spot.

NARRATOR: Off the field, Forbes and Sportico publish roughly $125 million worldwide off-field estimates for 2026. Their definitions and periods differ; these are estimates, not disclosed U.S. fees.³

*Graphic: "$125M, 2026 estimate, worldwide. Paid to Ohtani, not to the team."*

ANALYST: Put the brands aside for a second. That money goes to him. What the team gets is the wins. Keep that in mind, because Greg didn't.

## [1:45] The first fumble

*Sepia flashback filter. Ticker: "FLASHBACK: FREE AGENCY."*

NARRATOR: Free agency. The Angels already knew him. They had the clubhouse, the fans and the parking spot with his name on it. All they had to do was say yes.

*Reenactment, Angels boardroom. Seven executives around a long table. GREG stands at the end holding a calculator upside down.*

GREG: Seven hundred million dollars. For a baseball player. Gentlemen, I've run the numbers.

EXEC: What do they say?

GREG: *(turns the calculator toward them. The display reads "0.00L")* They say "no."

*Record scratch. An intern drops a coffee.*

EXEC: Did you read the payment schedule?

GREG: I read the first number. The first number was enough.

NARRATOR: According to our entirely made-up insiders, Greg's analysis stopped at the headline. He never opened page two.

*Close-up on a stapled contract. Page two still has the staple's original crease.*

## [3:00] The deal Greg missed

*Clean studio. A giant animated "$700M" breaks apart into pieces.*

NARRATOR: Here's what was on page two. Ohtani deferred $680 million of the $700 million. He takes $2 million a year while he plays. The rest arrives in $68 million payments from 2034 to 2043, with no interest.⁴

*Graphic: "Present value, AP luxury-tax method: about $461M. Luxury-tax charge: about $46M a year."⁵*

ANALYST: Money paid in 2043 costs less than money paid today. AP reported about $461 million under the prescribed luxury-tax valuation method, or about $46 million in annual CBT payroll charge. That is not today's remaining liability, actual cash tax, or our original-decision payment DCF. The club also has deferred-compensation funding obligations. Greg read the headline and skipped the definitions.

ANCHOR: So what did the Dodgers do?

ANALYST: They signed him before Greg found his reading glasses.

*Confetti cannon. One piece lands in ANCHOR's coffee. ANCHOR drinks it anyway.*

## [4:15] Enter Fred

*Lights dim. A server rack hums. Blue LEDs blink in sequence.*

NARRATOR: Back at our agency, a new hire was reviewing the books. His name is Fred. Fred is an accounting bot. Fred has never taken a lunch break, because Fred doesn't know what lunch is.

FRED: Initiating audit of predecessor's work.

*A filing drawer slides open by itself. Folders spill onto the floor. One folder is labeled "VIBES."*

FRED: Predecessor's model contains one spreadsheet. The spreadsheet contains one cell. The cell reads "Too much."

*Long pause. Fans whir louder.*

FRED: There are no formulas. There is a coffee ring. Recalculating.

## [5:15] Fred runs the numbers

*Fred's "face," a monitor with two dots and a line, turns into a spreadsheet.*

FRED: Step one. Price of a win. FanGraphs estimates about $13.9 million per projected win for the 2025 free-agent cohort projected at two or more WAR, using its multi-year and inflation assumptions.⁶

FRED: Step two. Ohtani's 2025 FanGraphs value was 9.4 wins above replacement, the best in the National League.²

*Graphic: "9.4 x $13.9M = about $131M. Retrospective market-cost proxy. Not profit or ten-year contract value."*

FRED: Using the unrounded $13.91 million rate, nine point four times thirteen point nine one is $130.754 million. That is an illustrative market-cost proxy applied to realized output, not cash income or proven contract value. The roughly $46 million CBT charge is a separate payroll measure.

ANALYST: Can we add every sponsorship dollar?

FRED: No. We need attributable retained club contribution and must avoid counting the same benefit twice. In 2024 the Dodgers added 12 Japan-based sponsors, about $70 million in estimated club sponsorship revenue growth in a secondhand report. Ohtani-specific causation and retained profit are unverified.⁷ In 2025 they drew 4,012,470 fans at home, a franchise record and the most in baseball.⁸ That count describes attendance, not incremental Ohtani sales or retained profit. I cannot assign the gains to one player without a credible comparison.

*Graphic: "12 Japan-based partners (2024, estimate). 4.01M home attendance (2025)."*

FRED: *(voice pitching up, LEDs flickering red)* Greg... passed... on... this?

*Sparks fly from the rack. A tiny robotic arm appears and sprays a tiny fire extinguisher at itself.*

FRED: I'm fine. That was an emotion. I have deleted it.

## [6:30] The second chance

*Ticker: "SOURCES: ANGELS GET SECOND SHOT."*

NARRATOR: In this fictional universe, the Angels got one more shot to bring Ohtani back to Anaheim.

*Boardroom again. GREG at a whiteboard that reads "$$$ = BAD" with a frowning face.*

GREG: History repeats itself. Too expensive.

EXEC: Greg, Fred's illustrative one-season market proxy is nearly three times the CBT payroll charge. Does that settle the contract?

FRED: No. A ten-year club return needs future contribution, all incremental costs and an alternative roster. The ratio is not profit.

GREG: Fred is a toaster.

*Speakerphone in the middle of the table crackles.*

FRED: *(speakerphone)* I am a certified controller. I also don't make toast. I checked.

GREG: Then what do you make?

FRED: Corrections.

NARRATOR: The Angels listened to Greg. Again.

*Cut to stadium crowd: boos. A hot dog flies past the camera in slow motion, mustard first.*

## [7:30] Fallout: the Ohio rumor

*Ticker: "ANGELS EXPLORING... OHIO?"*

ANCHOR: Fictional sources tell us a frustrated Angels front office is now "exploring opportunities in Ohio."

*EXEC stands at the edge of a cornfield in a suit, holding a folded map upside down.*

EXEC: Is Columbus nice in April?

*A tractor drives past. EXEC waves. The farmer doesn't wave back.*

ANALYST: *(back in studio)* If your accountant costs you the best player on Earth twice, you start looking at maps. Usually other people's maps.

ANCHOR: Has anyone told Ohio?

ANALYST: Ohio has not been told.

## [8:15] Fans react

*Street interviews outside a ballpark. Handheld camera.*

FAN 1: Greg cost us Shohei. Twice. Revoke his parking spot. Then paint over it.

FAN 2: I'd let Fred do my taxes. I'd let Fred raise my kids. Fred would remember their birthdays.

FAN 3: *(wearing a "GREG WAS RIGHT" shirt)* I made this before the numbers came out.

*FAN 3 turns the shirt inside out. The inside reads "FRED."*

FAN 4: *(a kid with a foam finger)* Who's Greg?

FAN 5: *(an older fan, patting the kid's head)* Exactly.

## [9:00] Greg's last day

*Grainy security-cam footage. Timestamp overlay. Hallway, 8:02 a.m.*

NARRATOR: Our CFO let Greg go. Greg came back the next morning and was last seen in the mail room.

*Blurred figure hugging a trash can next to the postage machine. A stack of envelopes slowly slides off a shelf.*

NARRATOR: Witnesses describe him as "very loud" and "not dry." We'll leave it there, out of respect for the mail room.

FRED: *(off-screen)* I have ordered new envelopes.

## [9:15] The Catalina incident

*Ticker: "DEVELOPING: GREG SPOTTED ON CATALINA ISLAND."*

ANCHOR: Days later, Greg turned up on Catalina Island, celebrating what he called his freedom.

TOURIST: *(sunburned, holding a shave ice)* He kept yelling "Too expensive!" at the ferry. The ferry didn't care.

NARRATOR: He headed down the Avalon dock toward a boat he believed was his. It was not his boat.

*Slow-motion slip. Crunch sound. Seagulls scatter. One seagull lands, steals his visor and flies off.*

NARRATOR: Greg broke his ankle and was airlifted to Hoag Hospital in Newport Beach.

FRED: Air ambulance plus emergency room. Estimated cost exceeds Greg's entire forecasting budget. He should have asked me first.

ANCHOR: Doctors expect a full recovery. Sources say Greg has already called the helicopter "overpriced."

## [9:40] Outro

*Studio. FRED's monitor now sits in the co-anchor chair, wearing a tiny tie.*

FRED: A contract is more than its price tag. We compare future retained club contribution and roster alternatives against compensation and other costs. Ticket counts, worldwide attention and player endorsements cannot replace that model. Greg read one number. I read the payment schedule.

ANCHOR: That's the Greg blunder. Fred, we salute you.

FRED: Saluting is not in my budget. I will nod.

*FRED's monitor dips one pixel. End card: "Subscribe for Part 3: GREG ON CRUTCHES."*

## [10:00] End

## Disclaimer

This is fictional parody. Greg, Fred, the Angels' second chance, the Ohio rumor, the mail room scene and the Catalina incident never happened. Greg is a good-natured exercise character, and nothing here describes any real person's conduct. Ohtani's contract, statistics and commercial figures are real and are cited below. Endorsement figures are worldwide estimates of Ohtani's personal income, not Dodgers revenue. The $131 million figure passed arithmetic review only. Its retrospective market-cost methodology cannot establish club profit or whole-contract value. The approximately $461 million figure is AP-reported CBT-method valuation, not today's liability. The extra scenes are optional fiction; no video has been generated.
