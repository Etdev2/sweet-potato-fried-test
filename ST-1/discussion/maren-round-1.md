# Maren Cedar, round 1

## Scope

I will turn reviewed evidence into three editable, exportable decks. Factual claims will carry source identifiers linked to notes. Numbers will distinguish reported results, estimates and scenarios. The Greg-versus-Fred deck will label its characters and events as fictional satire on every slide. It will make no claims about real people.

## Atomic tasks

1. Assemble a claim ledger with audience, claim, source, date, uncertainty and slide destination. Return unsupported claims to their content owner.
2. Build three 16:9 PowerPoint decks and matching PDFs after the captain settles the open content decisions. Include readable citations and speaker notes.
3. Check slide overflow, contrast, numeric consistency, source links and PDF rendering. Deliver editable files, PDFs, sources and a short QA record.

## Audience, format and sequence proposals

Owner and agency leadership receive an eight-slide contract-worth decision case: decision sought; business problem; documented contribution; contract economics; alternatives; sensitivity and limits; recommended terms; decision and evidence appendix. Use restrained navy, white and one accent. Financial charts must expose assumptions.

U.S. advertisers receive an eight-slide campaign pitch: advertiser objective; audience evidence; campaign idea; example execution; inventory and rights; measurement plan; indicative package; next decision and sources. Use a distinct campaign accent and concrete creative examples approved by Carolina.

The personal internal audience receives a six-slide fictional satire: fiction notice; invented rivalry; Greg's pitch; Fred's arithmetic; absurd negotiation; fictional resolution. Use illustrated characters and obviously invented props, with no real financial or performance claims.
