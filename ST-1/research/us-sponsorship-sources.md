## Hypothetical advertiser breakeven

This teaching scenario uses invented assumptions. It is not a published Ohtani fee, a brand forecast, a valuation of any agreement above, or a guaranteed return. Assume a one-year U.S. campaign costs $3 million for rights and $2 million for media, creative, and other activation. Total campaign cost is $5 million. All amounts are USD.

Use incremental U.S. net sales relative to the estimated outcome without the campaign. Contribution margin must subtract product, fulfillment, returns, payment, and other variable costs. Do not count campaign costs in that margin again.

Breakeven incremental net sales = total campaign cost / contribution margin.

| Hypothetical contribution margin | Incremental U.S. net sales required to cover $5 million |
| --- | --- |
| 20% | $25 million |
| 25% | $20 million |
| 40% | $12.5 million |

At 25%, $10 million incremental net sales produces $2.5 million contribution and a $2.5 million campaign loss. $20 million breaks even. $30 million produces $7.5 million contribution and a $2.5 million campaign profit. At $40 incremental contribution per order, breakeven requires 125,000 additional orders.

Estimate the increment with a randomized holdout or a justified geographic comparison, allowing for existing demand, other promotions, distribution changes, cannibalization, and spillover. Recorded sales and impressions alone do not establish that increment. Include repeat purchases only when measured over the defined horizon. Discount longer-term cash flows rather than mixing periods.
